# Host organisations for Open Touring

Researched 16 October 2026 from primary sources only. Every claim carries a URL. "Unverified" means the primary source did not state it or could not be read. Nothing was sent to any organisation.

**Question.** Which existing Australian not-for-profit could auspice Open Touring (OTWD), holding the data, name and code for the project with a right to transfer to a successor, with a project steering group, insurance that covers publishing user-contributed content, stated fees and exit terms. Order of preference given: OSGeo Oceania, then Linux Australia, then Open Source Collective (money only).

## 1. OSGeo Oceania

### Legal form and registration

- OSGeo Oceania Limited, an Australian public company limited by guarantee, ACN 633 200 272, ABN 18 633 200 272. ABN active from 1 May 2019, GST registered, main business location TAS 7008. Source: [ABN Lookup record](https://abr.business.gov.au/ABN/View?abn=18633200272).
- The same ABN Lookup record shows "Not entitled to receive tax deductible gifts" and lists no ACNC registration and no charity tax concessions. So it is a not-for-profit company, not a registered charity, and gifts to it are not deductible. Source: [ABN Lookup record](https://abr.business.gov.au/ABN/View?abn=18633200272). Cross-check on the ACNC register was not possible (the search page is script-driven), so treat "not an ACNC charity" as the ABN Lookup reading, not a direct ACNC confirmation.
- The company's own constitution names it "A Company Limited by Guarantee not having Share Capital", with a $1 member guarantee (clause 6), at least 3 and at most 9 directors (clause 73). Source: [Constitution, 7 Nov 2019](https://osmfoundation.org/w/images/0/0a/OSGeo_Oceania_Company_Constitution_20191107.pdf). The wiki also links the same constitution from [wiki.osgeo.org/wiki/Oceania](https://wiki.osgeo.org/wiki/Oceania) (Google Drive copy).
- Registered place of business on the 2020 OSMF chapter application: Floor 2, 18 Elizabeth Street, Hobart, Tasmania. Source: [OSMF local chapter application](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Local_Chapters/Applications/Oceania). That page was last edited 8 August 2020, so the address may be out of date (unverified).

### Governing documents and policies

- Constitution, objects (clause 2.1): "To support and grow the open geospatial community in the Oceania region, including the oversight and stewardship of a FOSS4G & SotM Conference series." Powers exist only so far as necessary or convenient to carry out that object (clause 4). Source: [Constitution](https://osmfoundation.org/w/images/0/0a/OSGeo_Oceania_Company_Constitution_20191107.pdf).
- Not-for-profit clauses: income and property must be applied solely to the objects (clause 7), no distribution to members (clause 8). On winding up, surplus goes to an institution with similar objects and an equivalent non-distribution constitution, chosen by special resolution of members or, failing that, a court (clauses 120 to 122). Source: [Constitution](https://osmfoundation.org/w/images/0/0a/OSGeo_Oceania_Company_Constitution_20191107.pdf).
- The board controls the company and its property (clause 84) and may delegate to committees of directors and members (clauses 100 to 102). Indemnity for officers is in clauses 114 to 117. Source: [Constitution](https://osmfoundation.org/w/images/0/0a/OSGeo_Oceania_Company_Constitution_20191107.pdf).
- Other documents listed on the wiki: Founding Terms of Reference (2018-12-17), Membership Policy, Finance Policy, Sponsorship Policy, Finance Committee Terms of Reference, Communications and Outreach Working Group Terms of Reference. Source: [wiki.osgeo.org/wiki/Oceania](https://wiki.osgeo.org/wiki/Oceania). The Finance Policy was not read for this report (unverified content).
- Sponsorship Policy: funds activities, not hosting. Tiers are microgrant (AUD 250 per application), small (AUD 1,000), high budget (AUD 5,000, board approval) and monthly donations (AUD 1,000 per year). It states the "activity must promote or be linked to open geospatial software/data/standards and/or the OSGeo Oceania community". Source: [Sponsorship Policy PDF](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1Aae8OlSFoZODTPvro-K53NYIe6_Hh1QM). Public summary: [osgeo-oceania.org/funding](https://osgeo-oceania.org/funding/).

### Hosting of outside projects

- Found no evidence that OSGeo Oceania has hosted an outside project or acts as a fiscal sponsor or auspice body. Its stated activities are the FOSS4G SotM Oceania conference series, local events, a grants program and working groups. Sources: [wiki.osgeo.org/wiki/Oceania](https://wiki.osgeo.org/wiki/Oceania), [osgeo-oceania.org](https://osgeo-oceania.org). A web search for fiscal sponsorship or hosting by the chapter found nothing.
- Its GitHub organisation holds conference websites and a Slack reporting tool, plus a fork of an outside project used for a 2019 conference site. Nothing resembling a hosted community project. Source: [github.com/osgeo-oceania](https://github.com/osgeo-oceania).
- It describes an "OSS Capabilities Map" as "a community-driven project by OSGeo Oceania" at [map.osgeo-oceania.org](https://map.osgeo-oceania.org). This is its own project, not an outside one.
- OSM special interest group: the wiki lists "OpenStreetMap Oceania" as a Special Interest Group and says it was not active as of February 2024. Source: [wiki.osgeo.org/wiki/Oceania](https://wiki.osgeo.org/wiki/Oceania). The chapter says it has a working group promoting OSM and is the "custodian of State of the Map within Oceania". Source: [osgeo-oceania.org/openstreetmap-osm-state-of-the-map-sotm/](https://osgeo-oceania.org/openstreetmap-osm-state-of-the-map-sotm/).
- OSGeo (global) incubation: the Incubation Committee lists geospatial open source projects, mentors them toward OSGeo project status and offers "some liability insurance for the project team" and access to OSGeo donations and its annual budget. Requirements include a geospatial open source licence, open governance and a code of conduct. Source: [osgeo.org incubation](https://www.osgeo.org/about/committees/incubation/). The page does not say whether OSGeo holds project assets. It is a software project pathway run by the global foundation, not by the Oceania chapter, and Open Touring is a database plus app, so fit is unverified. OSGeo (global) has a US not-for-profit and a Dutch stichting, per [osgeo.org/about](https://www.osgeo.org/about/). Chapters are listed as a body, but the page does not say how a chapter relates legally or financially to the foundation. OSGeo Oceania is a separate Australian company.

Source: [OSMF chapter application](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Local_Chapters/Applications/Oceania), [wiki.osgeo.org/wiki/Oceania](https://wiki.osgeo.org/wiki/Oceania).
- A Finance Policy and a Finance Committee exist (see above). Their terms were not read.

- Nothing stated on cover for publishing user-contributed content.

### Decision making

- Board of nine, elected by members in annual elections. 2026 board: Dani Bramante (Chair), Nick Forbes-Smith (Deputy Chair), Alex Leith (Secretary), Jonah Sullivan (Treasurer), Ana Belgun, Ewen Hill, Phoebe Adams, Dean Howell, Aleksandar Zivaljevic. Source: [board page](https://osgeo-oceania.org/board-of-directors/), [wiki.osgeo.org/wiki/Oceania](https://wiki.osgeo.org/wiki/Oceania).
- Board motions are recorded on Loomio: [loomio.org/osgeo-oceania](https://www.loomio.org/osgeo-oceania). Board discussion happens on the public `oceania-board` mailing list. Source: [osgeo-oceania.org/about](https://osgeo-oceania.org/about/).
- The Grants Group (at least one director plus registered members) decides small tiers, the board decides the AUD 5,000 tier. Source: [Sponsorship Policy PDF](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1Aae8OlSFoZODTPvro-K53NYIe6_Hh1QM).

### Public contact route

- admin@osgeo-oceania.org, listed on [wiki.osgeo.org/wiki/Oceania](https://wiki.osgeo.org/wiki/Oceania) and [osgeo-oceania.org/about](https://osgeo-oceania.org/about/). Public lists `oceania` and `oceania-board` are also listed. The board page itself publishes no address. Joining as a member is free via [osgeo-oceania.org/members/application-for-membership](https://osgeo-oceania.org/members/application-for-membership/).

### Possible blockers

- Objects are narrow: "open geospatial community in the Oceania region" plus conference stewardship. A touring and campsite database with an app may count as geospatial open data, but the board would have to agree that holding it is within clause 2.1 and clause 4 powers. Unverified, and the likeliest block.
- No precedent, no policy and no template for holding another project's assets, name or code. Everything would be bespoke.
- No insurance reported, and not a registered charity, so no DGR route for donors.
- Volunteer board with one object and an events focus, so capacity is a risk. The OSM SIG is described as inactive.
- No stated view on data licences. The Sponsorship Policy favours "open data", which is consistent with CC BY 4.0 but not a position on it.
- No stated policy on commercial sponsors beyond the sponsorship policy. Unverified.

## 2. Linux Australia

### Legal form and registration

- Linux Australia Inc., "Other Incorporated Entity", ABN 56 987 117 479, active from 1 November 1999, GST registered, location NSW 2204. Registered as a charity with the ACNC from 9 April 2025. Charity tax concession: income tax exemption from 9 April 2025. Business name "Open Source Australia" from 24 November 2017. Source: [ABN Lookup record](https://abr.business.gov.au/ABN/View?abn=56987117479). DGR status was not visible in the extracted record (unverified).
- Site footer gives NSW registration Y2998126 and ARBN 618 108 544. Source: [linux.org.au](https://linux.org.au).
- Direct ACNC register page was not readable (timed out). Charity status is from ABN Lookup.

### Governing documents and policies

- Constitution adopted at a Special General Meeting on 9 April 2025 (commit 9e59271). Objects include "to support open technologies" and open technologies advocacy. No pecuniary gain to members (AdmNfp-1). Surplus on winding up goes to an organisation with similar objects not run for members' profit (AdmWin-1). The committee may establish subcommittees (ComDel-1) and may take out insurance for the association's assets and liabilities (AdmIns-1). Committee of 7 (ComCom-1). Only individuals can be members (MemGen-2), entrance fee $1 and annual subscription $2, or other amounts set by the committee. Source: [linux.org.au/about-us/constitution](https://linux.org.au/about-us/constitution/). Source repository: [github.com/linuxaustralia/constitution_and_policies](https://github.com/linuxaustralia/constitution_and_policies).
- Subcommittee Policy, current version in the official repository is v3, dated October 2019 in its text: [subcommittee_policy_v3.md](https://github.com/linuxaustralia/constitution_and_policies/blob/master/subcommittee_policy_v3.md). The repository lists no later version and a `superseded_policies` folder. The public policies page also lists an Insurance policy dated 12 September 2019: [linux.org.au/about-us/policies](https://linux.org.au/about-us/policies/).
- Also in the repository: Grant and Sponsorship Policy, Conflict of Interest Policy, Expense Policy, Financial Procedure Guide, Privacy Policy, Code of Conduct. See the repository link above.

### How subcommittees work (v3)

Quoted or closely paraphrased from [subcommittee_policy_v3.md](https://github.com/linuxaustralia/constitution_and_policies/blob/master/subcommittee_policy_v3.md):

- Five types: Association (no money handling), Independent (own funds, held in Linux Australia's bank account), Event, Steering (recurring events), Operational (services to LA). A subcommittee "is a part of Linux Australia" and is bound by its constitution and policies.
- Creation: only current LA members can establish one, by emailing council@linux.org.au. The usual turnaround is within 28 days.
- Independent subcommittees spend their own funds within their stated objectives. Funded types may only incur liabilities in their approved budget, pre-authorised in writing by Council. No overdrafts.
- Insurance: "available to all subcommittees", event insurance is self-funded.
- Liability: independent subcommittees are personally liable for unauthorised expenses. Others can face personal liability for loss or overspend.
- Assets on wind-up: "all subcommittee assets revert to Linux Australia's general asset pool", restorable if the group reforms within two years. Independent subcommittees "may transfer assets to another entity whose charter aligns with LA's objects".
- Wind-up: by 75% member vote, or by Council on 31 days' notice. Stale registry data (over 184 days out of date) is an automatic trigger.
- Fees: events receiving LA funding return a "Community Contribution", "currently set at 6%" of gross profit. Steering and Operational budgets carry a "Return to Linux Australia" line. No fee is stated for an Independent subcommittee.
- IP and branding: the policy "does not address" intellectual property or trademarks. Ownership of data, name and code would need a separate written agreement.

### Hosting of outside projects

Linux Australia lists these subcommittees and community groups: Admin, Mirror Team, Everything Open, PyCon AU, DrupalSouth Steering Committee, WordPress Steering Committee (WordCamps), Python New Zealand (Kiwi PyCon), Joomla Australia, Linux Users Victoria (under review) and Media and Communications. Source: [linux.org.au/get-involved/sub-committees](https://linux.org.au/get-involved/sub-committees/). Several are outside community bodies for other projects (Drupal, WordPress, Joomla, Python NZ). Almost all are events or infrastructure, with no data or app project listed. Some entries look out of date (the page itself flags the DrupalSouth member list). The page invites proposals with a name, team, purpose, expected duration, funding needs and community engagement plan.

### Fees and financial arrangements

- 6% Community Contribution on LA-funded event profit, as above. Source: [subcommittee_policy_v3.md](https://github.com/linuxaustralia/constitution_and_policies/blob/master/subcommittee_policy_v3.md).
- Grants: Project Grants fund "open source, open data, open government, open education, open hardware and open culture projects", with resources produced required to be "publicly available under an acceptable open licence". The pool depends on prior-year profit and applications close mid-November. Source: [grant_and_sponsorhip_policy.md](https://github.com/linuxaustralia/constitution_and_policies/blob/master/grant_and_sponsorhip_policy.md).

### Insurance

- "Linux Australia will generally cover the insurance liability of Linux Australia conferences in Australia" and offers cover for reasonable community events. Source: [linux.org.au/about-us/policies](https://linux.org.au/about-us/policies/). The policy and constitution say nothing about cover for an online publishing service, media or defamation liability, or user-contributed content. Unverified whether the insurer would extend to it. This is the key question to put to Council.

### Decision making

- A 7-member committee ("Council") controls the association (ComFun-1, ComCom-1) and approves subcommittee budgets and liabilities. Members vote at general meetings, including 75% votes to wind up a subcommittee. Meets generally every 2 weeks, minutes are public. Sources: [constitution](https://linux.org.au/about-us/constitution/), [policies page](https://linux.org.au/about-us/policies/), [subcommittee policy v3](https://github.com/linuxaustralia/constitution_and_policies/blob/master/subcommittee_policy_v3.md).
- Conflicts of interest are handled by a general policy that does not mention sponsors or commercial deals specifically. Source: [conflict-of-interest-policy.md](https://github.com/linuxaustralia/constitution_and_policies/blob/master/conflict-of-interest-policy.md).

### Public contact route

- council@linux.org.au, and a contact form at [linux.org.au/contact](https://linux.org.au/contact/) (replies typically 3 to 5 business days). Subcommittee proposals go to council@linux.org.au per [sub-committees page](https://linux.org.au/get-involved/sub-committees/) and policy v3. Membership is free: [linux.org.au/membership](https://linux.org.au/membership/).

### Possible blockers

- Structural conflict with "right to transfer to a successor". Default rule is that subcommittee assets revert to LA's pool. Only the Independent type has a stated route to transfer to an aligned entity, and LA decides. Any name, data or code transfer right would need to be written into a side agreement.
- The subcommittee is "part of Linux Australia", so LA's Council carries final authority over budgets, liabilities and wind-up. A project steering group would sit inside that structure, not above it.
- Constitution objects are about open technologies, and the grant policy explicitly includes open data. Fit looks good, but whether a crowd-sourced consumer database is a "subcommittee" use case is unverified.
- Insurance statements cover events, not online publishing (see above).
- Policy v3 is dated 2019 and the constitution was reissued in 2025, so check with Council whether a newer subcommittee policy is in draft (unverified).

## 3. Open Source Collective

### Legal form and registration

- Open Source Collective is a US 501(c)(6) non-profit, EIN 82-2037583. Contributions to hosted Collectives are not tax-deductible as charitable contributions for US federal income tax. Source: [opencollective.com/opensource](https://opencollective.com/opensource), [oscollective.org](https://www.oscollective.org/). No Australian registration (it is not an Australian entity, so ACN, ABN and ACNC do not apply).

### Governing documents

- Terms of Fiscal Sponsorship Agreement, governed by California law, disputes through mediation then binding arbitration (section 8, section 10). Source: [Terms (published Google Doc)](https://docs.google.com/document/u/1/d/e/2PACX-1vQbiyK2Fe0jLdh4vb9BfHY4bJ1LCo4Qvy0jg9P29ZkiC8y_vKJ_1fNgIbV0p6UdvbcT8Ql1gVto8bf9/pub), summary at [docs.oscollective.org terms page](https://docs.oscollective.org/welcome-and-introduction-to-osc/terms-of-fiscal-sponsorship).
- Acceptance criteria: original or substantial fork, recent activity or significant usage, applicant with standing, an open source licence (OSI, FSF, Debian, Fedora or OSHWA standards), and an organisation repository, not a personal account. Source: [acceptance criteria](https://docs.oscollective.org/getting-started/acceptance-criteria). Open Touring's code licences (AGPL-3.0 and Apache-2.0) are OSI-approved. The CC BY 4.0 data licence is not addressed by the criteria (unverified).

### Hosting of outside projects

- Hosting outside open source projects is its whole function. Services listed: accounting, taxes, invoices, contracts, employment, expense processing and bank account management, "worldwide" payees. Source: [oscollective.org](https://www.oscollective.org/), [is OSC right for me](https://docs.oscollective.org/interested-in-joining-osc/is-osc-right-for-me).

### Fees

- A fixed 10% fee on incoming funds from external donors. No setup, minimum or recurring fees. Stripe, PayPal and Wise processing fees are extra. Funds are held in USD. Source: [fees](https://docs.oscollective.org/welcome-and-introduction-to-osc/fees), [is OSC right for me](https://docs.oscollective.org/interested-in-joining-osc/is-osc-right-for-me).
- Projects expecting under USD 600 a year in donations may be better served elsewhere. Source: [is OSC right for me](https://docs.oscollective.org/interested-in-joining-osc/is-osc-right-for-me).

### Assets, name and exit

- "you retain ownership of all your project's intellectual property." OSC "can hold assets (including trademarks) on behalf of your project". Source: [trademarks and IP](https://docs.oscollective.org/for-hosted-member-projects/trademarks-and-ip).
- Exit: either party may terminate on 30 days' written notice, and within that period the project may name a successor to receive the remaining fund and "Other Assets" (section 6). OSC says there is "no contractual obligation to stay" and will transfer funds and assets to another nonprofit host if the money keeps supporting open source. It may hold back funds for outstanding contracts. Source: [Terms](https://docs.google.com/document/u/1/d/e/2PACX-1vQbiyK2Fe0jLdh4vb9BfHY4bJ1LCo4Qvy0jg9P29ZkiC8y_vKJ_1fNgIbV0p6UdvbcT8Ql1gVto8bf9/pub), [closing a collective](https://docs.oscollective.org/for-hosted-member-projects/closing-a-collective). Trademarks can move to a successor that meets legal criteria, and lapse if there is none. Source: [trademarks and IP](https://docs.oscollective.org/for-hosted-member-projects/trademarks-and-ip).
- Note the Terms say other assets "shall become the property of OSC" while a successor is being named. This reads in tension with the IP-retention statement and needs clarification in writing. Source: [Terms section 3(E)](https://docs.google.com/document/u/1/d/e/2PACX-1vQbiyK2Fe0jLdh4vb9BfHY4bJ1LCo4Qvy0jg9P29ZkiC8y_vKJ_1fNgIbV0p6UdvbcT8Ql1gVto8bf9/pub).

### Insurance

- Neither the Terms, the governance page nor the FAQ addresses insurance. The Collective "is responsible for all activities of its Project, including any financial or other liabilities" and must indemnify OSC (section 14). User-generated content is not addressed. Source: [Terms](https://docs.google.com/document/u/1/d/e/2PACX-1vQbiyK2Fe0jLdh4vb9BfHY4bJ1LCo4Qvy0jg9P29ZkiC8y_vKJ_1fNgIbV0p6UdvbcT8Ql1gVto8bf9/pub), [governance and mediation](https://docs.oscollective.org/for-hosted-member-projects/governance-and-mediation). So there is no cover for publishing user content, and the liability sits with the project.

### Decision making

- Project admins make the decisions. OSC does not mediate or arbitrate, and "will freeze your account at the first instance" of an admin access loss from governance breakdown or a security incident. Source: [governance and mediation](https://docs.oscollective.org/for-hosted-member-projects/governance-and-mediation). Keep multiple admins on the collective.

### Public contact route

- hello@oscollective.org, the application at [opencollective.com/opensource/apply](https://opencollective.com/opensource/apply), Discord and booked calls. Source: [opencollective.com/opensource](https://opencollective.com/opensource), [oscollective.org](https://www.oscollective.org/).

### Possible blockers

- Money and bookkeeping only. It will not act as the Australian legal body for contracts, domains, consumer-law exposure or publisher liability in any practical sense, and no insurance is stated.
- No Australian charity or tax status. Australian donors get nothing tax-wise, and USD holding adds currency cost.
- The project would still need its own steering structure and admins. OSC will not set one up.
- The 10% fee applies to external donations, and the USD 600 guide is a soft floor.
- The Terms ownership wording (3(E)) needs clarifying before relying on it for asset retention.

## Recommendation

Approach OSGeo Oceania first, as the closest community match, but go in with narrow questions and a firm four-week reply window. In parallel, prepare the Linux Australia subcommittee proposal, because it is the only one of the three with a published hosting mechanism and a charity status. If OSGeo Oceania cannot say yes to scope, insurance and a transfer right, move to Linux Australia. Add Open Source Collective only if donations need a home.

**Ask OSGeo Oceania (admin@osgeo-oceania.org, copy the public board list or a Loomio proposal):**

1. Does the board consider a touring and campsite open data project within the object in constitution clause 2.1 (open geospatial community in Oceania)? If so, would it pass a board motion to host it.
2. Will it hold the name, domain, data and code in trust for the project, with a written right to transfer all of them to a successor body on a stated notice period?
3. Will it delegate day-to-day decisions to a project steering group by committee under clause 100, with named project representatives?
4. What insurance does it hold today, and would the insurer extend public liability and media or publisher liability to a user-contributed content service? If not, who would carry it.
5. What does it charge, if anything, and how would money received for the project be ring-fenced?
6. Exit terms, and how a board change or a winding up (clauses 120 to 122) would affect the project's assets.

**Ask Linux Australia (council@linux.org.au) if the above fails:**

1. Will Council accept an Independent subcommittee, and will it agree in writing that assets (data, name, domain, code) are held for the project and may be transferred to a successor body whose charter aligns with LA's objects.
2. Does the event and community-event insurance extend to an online publishing service with user-contributed content. If not, what is the cover.
3. Whether any fee applies beyond the 6% event contribution.
4. Whether a newer subcommittee policy than v3 is in draft.
5. Who on Council would sponsor the proposal. Every member of the proposing group needs to be an LA member first (free).

**Ask Open Source Collective only for the money role:** confirm in writing that the 3(E) "Other Assets" wording does not transfer ownership of project data or code, and confirm that the CC BY 4.0 data licence alongside AGPL and Apache code is acceptable.

**Cross-cutting point for all three.** None of them states insurance that covers publishing user-contributed content. Open Touring should get its own quote for public liability and media or cyber liability and ask each host to be noted on, or to name the project on, that policy.
