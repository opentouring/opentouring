# Road restrictions, road segments and ferry services as record types

OTWD records three record types beside sites: road restrictions (points where a physical or legal limit applies to vehicles, such as a low rail bridge or a bridge with a weight limit), road segments (stretches of road or track with line geometry, recorded for surface, vehicle access class, towing and seasonal closures) and ferry services (crossings between two ferry terminal sites, with the vehicle classes they carry, vehicle limits and fares). Ferry terminals are sites, with a new `ferry_terminal` site type. Travellers towing caravans need these facts to reach the sites OTWD already holds, and no open dataset brings them together for touring.

They are separate record types rather than new site types because they are not places a traveller stops at. A low bridge has no opening hours, prices or facilities, a road segment is a line, and a ferry service joins two sites. Folding them into Site would make most site facts meaningless for them and force line geometry onto every site. Each record type has its own schema, its own facts and its own public ID kind (`otwd:road_restriction:`, `otwd:road_segment:`, `otwd:ferry_service:`), and they all share one set of definitions: the fact wrapper and provenance ([ADR 0013](0013-stable-public-ids-and-per-fact-provenance.md)), status with closures and merges, derived values, the compact form, the change feed and submissions ([ADR 0009](0009-event-sourced-submissions-never-direct-writes.md)).

A road restriction's location and every limit on it (vertical clearance, width, weight and length), a road segment's geometry, vehicle access class, towing permitted, seasonal closure and limits, and a ferry service's geometry, accepted vehicle classes, ferry vehicle limits and seasonal closure are safety facts, so routing always sends a change to them to a moderator ([ADR 0010](0010-ai-recommends-fixed-code-routes-humans-decide.md)). Moving a low bridge to the wrong road, rerouting a 4WD-only track or shifting a ferry crossing is as hazardous as a wrong limit, so location and geometry are safety facts here, while a site's location stays an ordinary fact. Accepted vehicle classes decide whether a car towing a caravan may board. New records of every record type join the always-human list, like new sites. The fixed checks work out whether a submission is on the list from its kind and from the safety fact markers in the record type's schema, so a routing rule can never put itself outside the list. Day-to-day road closures are never facts, for the same reason declared fire bans are not ([ADR 0026](0026-fire-policy-not-fire-bans.md)): a seasonal closure links to the closing authority, and a temporary closure of a record records its authority and link.

## Considered options

- **New site types for bridges, tracks and ferries.** Rejected: most site facts would not apply, and sites are points.
- **One generic record with a free-form type field.** Rejected: facts could not be checked per type, and partner apps would have to guess which facts a record can carry.
- **A new submission kind per record type for every action.** Rejected: only creation needs type-specific required facts. Edits, closures, merges and problem reports name the record in `record_id`, whose kind segment already gives the type, so they stay one kind each.

## Consequences

Vertical clearance and width are lists of signed, surveyed or measured figures, per direction or lane where sources give them, because Australian road authorities publish all of these and a single number would lose the difference between the legal limit and the physical one. Apps and rig filters compare a vehicle with `lowest_m`, the lowest figure of any basis, with `lowest_basis` saying which kind it is: a measured figure below the sign is the one that strikes the vehicle, so the signed figure alone is never enough. Every height and width is capped at 10 m, so a figure in feet fails validation instead of passing as metres. Road segments keep `weight_limit` and `length_limit`, and road restrictions keep `length_limit`, because some roads carry a weight limit along their whole length and some passes are signed with a length limit, and a point record cannot describe a limit that applies along a whole stretch. Towing permitted comes only from contributors, who report the signs and conditions on the ground, and the schema rejects seed source provenance for it. A structure records whether the road passes over, under or through it. Ferry fares use vehicle bands that include the drawbar, as operators price them. Every submission, change, verdict and merge names its record in `record_id` whatever the record type, and photos can be of any record, such as the clearance sign at a road restriction. Seed sources for Victoria are Vicmap Transport and Road Bridges, both CC BY 4.0. The VicRoads height clearance dataset is not used until its licence is confirmed.
